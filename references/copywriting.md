# Copywriting — UI text that doesn't read as AI

Copy is design. Generic copy makes a well-built screen still feel generated. The tells and the fixes.

## Bans (rewrite on sight)
- **Buzzwords:** streamline, empower, supercharge, leverage, unleash, seamless, world-class, enterprise-grade, next-generation, cutting-edge, game-changer, mission-critical, revolutionary. Replace with a concrete noun + a verb that says what it literally does.
- **Aphoristic cadence as the house voice:** "Serious statement. Then a punchy negation." If three+ blocks land on a short rebuttal-shaped sentence, rewrite. Be specific, not quotable.
- **Em dashes as a tic** and the "not X — but Y" reflex. Use commas, colons, periods.
- **Restated headings / intros that repeat the title.** Every line earns its place.
- **ALL-CAPS sentences.** Caps only for short labels (≤4 words) and badges.

## Headlines
- Say the one specific thing this page does for this reader. Concrete beats clever.
- One idea per headline. If it needs an em dash to hold two ideas, split it.
- Test at every breakpoint — a headline that wraps to 5 lines on mobile is a layout bug.

## Buttons & links
- **Verb + object:** "Save changes" beats "OK"; "Delete project" beats "Yes"; "Get your token" beats "Submit". The label says what will happen.
- Link text must stand alone: "View pricing" beats "Click here" (screen readers announce links out of context).
- Primary action gets the strong label; secondary stays quiet ("Cancel", "Back").

## Microcopy that carries weight
- **Empty states:** say what goes here + the one action to fill it. Not "No data."
- **Errors:** what happened + how to fix it, in plain language. Never a raw code or "Something went wrong" alone.
- **Placeholders:** show the format ("you@example.com"), don't restate the label, and never use them as the only label.
- **Reassurance at friction points:** next to a signup or payment, one short line that removes the fear ("No card required", "Cancel anytime").

## Numbers and proof
- Specific numbers read as true: "123 products", "1,642 sections" beat "lots of".
- Don't inflate. One honest, checkable number outperforms three vague superlatives.

## The gate
Read the screen's copy out loud. If it sounds like a press release or a landing-page template, it's slop. Rewrite until it sounds like a person who knows the product telling you exactly what it does.
